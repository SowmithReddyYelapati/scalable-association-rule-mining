01 · Dataset Upload Upload a transactional CSV file or use the built-in Online Retail dataset to get started immediately.
02 · Transaction Preprocessing Raw rows are cleaned and grouped by transaction ID. Each transaction becomes a set of product identifiers. Cancelled orders and missing values are removed.
03 · Frequent Itemset Generation Apriori scans all transactions to find product combinations that appear in at least minimum support fraction of transactions.
04 · Candidate Generation & Pruning The Apriori property: every subset of a frequent itemset must also be frequent. This allows pruning of infrequent candidates early to reduce computation.
05 · Association Rule Generation Each frequent itemset is split into antecedent (IF) and consequent (THEN) parts. Only rules satisfying minimum confidence are kept.
06 · Metric Calculation Support, confidence and lift are calculated for every rule. Results are ranked by lift — the strongest associations appear first.
APRIORI ALGORITHM

How Apriori works

Apriori is a classic algorithm for frequent itemset mining, first proposed by Agrawal & Srikant (1994). It uses the Apriori property to systematically reduce the search space.

Count how often each individual product appears across all transactions. Products appearing in fewer than min_support × |T| transactions are discarded.

k = 1

Combine pairs of frequent (k-1)-itemsets that share a common prefix to produce candidate k-itemsets.

k → k+1

Any candidate whose (k-1)-item subsets are not all frequent is removed before counting. This dramatically reduces the number of candidates to evaluate.

Prune

Scan the transaction database to count the actual support of each remaining candidate. Candidates below min_support are eliminated.

Count

Increment k and repeat steps 2–4 until no new frequent itemsets can be formed. All collected frequent itemsets are returned.

Repeat

For each frequent itemset, generate all possible antecedent → consequent splits. Rules satisfying min_confidence are kept. Lift is calculated for each rule.

RULE METRICS

Support · Confidence · Lift

Three key metrics are calculated for every association rule.

Support Measures how frequently an itemset appears across all transactions. High support means the combination is very common. Support(A∪B) = freq(A∪B) / |T|
Confidence Among transactions containing A, the fraction that also contain B. Measures the reliability of the rule A → B. Confidence(A→B) = Support(A∪B) / Support(A)
Lift Measures how much stronger the association is compared to independent occurrence. Lift > 1 means positive association. Lift(A→B) = Confidence(A→B) / Support(B)
EXAMPLE

Reading an association rule

An association rule has the form: Antecedent → Consequent

Bread + Butter → Milk

Bread, Butter and Milk appear together in 4.25% of all transactions.

Among customers who buy Bread and Butter, 68.40% also buy Milk.

Customers who buy Bread and Butter are 1.72× more likely to also buy Milk than a random customer. This is a positive, useful association.
